## The 6-year LLC horizon

You named a goal: be in a *healthy position to form an LLC 6 years out.* Here's the CFO read, held at both poles:

**Conservative pole — what "healthy" means on paper, and what an LLC actually buys you:**
- An LLC is **liability protection + tax flexibility**, not a growth lever. The thing that makes you "ready" is **clean books with a verifiable revenue history** — which is exactly why I want a ledger (even a free one) from dollar one. Six years of clean records is what lets you convert cleanly, raise if you ever want to, or sell.
- **You may not want to wait 6 years.** The moment you have a non-founder operator handling other people's home networks, you have **liability exposure** (someone's network, someone's data, a contractor on someone's property). At that point an LLC is cheap insurance — formation is **$50–500 depending on state.** I'd flag forming it *when operator #2 or your first non-founder operator appears,* not on a fixed 6-year clock. The 6-year horizon is fine as a "fully mature business" target; the liability trigger may come sooner.

**Visionary pole — the structure question worth holding open:**
- You're two sole proprietors building a *guild with cooperative DNA* and a potential native token. A standard single-member LLC is the obvious default — but it's worth knowing that **a multi-member LLC, an LLC taxed as an S-corp, or even a cooperative structure** could each align differently with the dual-hat operator model. The Alliance-coin question (FIN-003) directly collides with entity choice — *you can't issue a token from an ambiguous structure.* **Both are true: form the simple LLC when liability demands it, AND keep the entity-structure question open as the guild's economics reveal themselves.** The CEO picks which pole to inhabit when the time comes.
