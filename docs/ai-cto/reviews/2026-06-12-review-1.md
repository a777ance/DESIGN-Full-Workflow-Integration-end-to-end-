### The productive tension on item #1

**Conservative pole — fix it now, fail closed.** A privacy product whose stated guarantee ("sensitive tasks never reach a cloud provider") is false-by-config is a credibility landmine. Defense-in-depth says the boundary must be enforced at every layer, not just the one the dispatcher can see. The fix is one line. Closing it costs nothing and removes a claim you can't currently make. This is compressed institutional memory: fail-closed is why we don't ship "fail-open and hope the local model stays up."

**Visionary pole — this layer shouldn't be in Phase 1 at all.** The entire AI-orchestration stack (LiteLLM, Odin, the console) is undeployed Phase-3 surface that grew while the Phase-1 critical path (nftables on the t630) sat blocked on access. The honest move might be to **freeze the orchestration layer**, slap a `STATUS: not a privacy guarantee — do not rely on` banner on its README, and spend zero further cycles there until 3 customers pay. The most rigorous fix for a fail-open privacy claim is sometimes to *delete the claim*, not patch the config.

**My recommendation:** do *both halves* of the cheap version — make the one-line config fix (so the artifact is correct if anyone runs it) AND add the "not-deployed, not-a-guarantee" banner — then freeze the layer. Five minutes total, and it stops the orchestration stack from quietly accruing more not-deployed surface while the real Phase-1 work waits on t630 access. The CEO decides which pole to inhabit; I'd lean conservative here because the cost of the fix is trivial and the downside of a false privacy claim is reputational.
