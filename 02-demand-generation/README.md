# 02 — Demand generation

**Lives in:** Google/Meta ads · the Google Business listing + the blog (for search) ·
Mailchimp (email).
**Go-live:** run one campaign aimed at one neighborhood's ZIP codes; build the email list
off the customer list (08), never a bought list.

The top of the funnel. Picture Jose's map of Maple Grove: three pins today, and the job is
to fill the block — not to scatter 30 pins across the whole metro that he'll spend his life
driving between. Two rules make that happen, and both are counter to how most local
businesses spend.

## Rule 1 — teach the problem before you sell the fix

Nobody wakes up wanting "encrypted DNS." But everybody understands *"something's watching
your TV and your kids' tablets, and we keep it out."* If you run bottom-of-funnel "buy now"
ads before people understand the problem, you're paying to teach them one at a time. So the
money goes into **teaching the problem at scale first** — plain-language posts and a strong
local listing built on the pest-control analogy. That work compounds; ads don't. Details and
real headlines in [`category-education.md`](category-education.md).

## Rule 2 — fill the block, don't chase the metro

A scattered customer wears the operator out — a whole afternoon lost to driving. A full
block is profitable, and one happy neighbor sells the next one over the fence. So we buy
ads **one neighborhood's ZIP codes at a time**, and the number we watch is **homes on the
route**, not impressions or clicks. Details and the worked Maple Grove example in
[`geo-targeting.md`](geo-targeting.md).

> The cheapest new customer there is is a current customer's word to the house next door —
> which is exactly why the **"refer a neighbor" ask lives inside the monthly statement** (06),
> not just on the website.

## Paid campaigns

Any paid push — Google, Meta, a flyer in the HOA newsletter — is aimed at **one route's ZIP
codes** and points at the homepage (01) → the booking form (03). If a campaign can't name
the neighborhood it's filling, it's buying reach, and we don't run it
([LAUNCH-NOTES #4](../LAUNCH-NOTES.md#4-geo-targeting-buys-reach-instead-of-route-density)).

## Hand-offs

- **← 00/01:** uses the brand voice; lands traffic on the website.
- **→ 03 funnels:** all traffic ends at the booking form.
- **↔ 08 customer list:** the email audience is pulled *from* the customer list; new leads
  are written back *to* it. We never import a bought or scraped list.
- **↔ 06 statements:** the "refer a neighbor" ask inside the statement feeds new leads
  back to the top.
