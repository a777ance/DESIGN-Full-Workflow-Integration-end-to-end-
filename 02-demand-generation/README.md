## Rule 1 — category before lead-gen

Nobody wakes up wanting encrypted DNS; everybody understands "someone's watching so the
bad stuff stays out." Spending on bottom-funnel lead-gen *before* the market understands
the category teaches one prospect at a time. So spend goes into **category education**
first (content + local SEO around the analogy), which teaches the market at scale and
compounds. Details in [`category-education.md`](category-education.md).

## Rule 2 — density before reach

A scattered customer exhausts the operator (a day lost to driving); a dense route is
profitable, and a happy neighbor refers the next neighbor, so density makes referrals
**compound**. Campaigns are therefore bought **zip-code-at-a-time**, and the unit metric
is **homes per route**, not impressions. This is the DoorDash-batching lesson applied to
acquisition. Details in [`geo-targeting.md`](geo-targeting.md).

> The cheapest density you can buy is a current customer's word to the house next door —
> which is why the **referral CTA is surfaced inside the monthly Statement** (06), not
> just on the website.

## Third-party campaigns

External/paid campaigns (Meta, Google, local print, HOA newsletters) are scoped to a
**single route's zips** and pointed at a landing page (01) → intake form (03). A campaign
that can't name the route it's filling is a campaign that's buying reach — see
[LAUNCH-NOTES #4](../LAUNCH-NOTES.md#4-geo-targeting-buys-reach-instead-of-route-density).

## Hand-offs

- **← 00/01:** inherits the brand voice and lands traffic on the storefront.
- **→ 03 funnels:** all traffic terminates at the intake form / booking.
- **↔ 08 CRM:** audiences are segmented *from* the system of record; new leads are
  written *back* to it. The list is never a scraped import.
- **↔ 06 statements:** the in-Statement referral CTA feeds new leads back to the top.
