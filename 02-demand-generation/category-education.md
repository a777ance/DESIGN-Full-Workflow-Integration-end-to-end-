# Category education — own the analogy

The market doesn't know it has a problem. Our job is to teach it — calmly, at scale, and in
a way nobody can copy because it's *ours*: **pest control for your home internet.** Win that
one analogy so completely that the category and our name become the same thought.

---

## The analogy, the whole strategy in one table

| Pest control | A777ance |
| ------------ | -------- |
| Bugs you never see | Trackers, ads following you around, the smart-TV phoning home, the odd scam link |
| You pay monthly even when you never see a bug | You pay monthly for the quiet — nothing going wrong |
| The sticker on the door proves someone came | The monthly **statement** proves the work happened |
| A licensed, bonded tech you trust in your house | Your vetted local **operator** (09) |

Every post, ad, and email is just one row of this table, expanded.

## The posts (these live on the blog, stage 01)

Real headlines, ready to brief a writer. Each one is plain-language, no scare tactics, and
ends with one button → the booking form (03).

1. **"Is your smart TV watching you back?"** — the invisible problem, made concrete. (It is,
   and we can show what it's sending.)
2. **"Why grandma's tablet is a target — and how to make it boring again."** — the
   elder-care angle. This is the most emotional version of "make the invisible visible," and
   it converts.
3. **"What your internet company can see about your house."** — in plain English, no jargon.
4. **"What a month of protection actually looks like."** — walk through a *real* statement,
   scan the QR, scroll it. The proof post.
5. **"Why you want a person, not an app."** — the trust pitch; quietly plants the seed that
   *you could do this too* and feeds the operator funnel (09).

### How a post should open (sample voice)

> You have more things on your Wi-Fi than you think. Count them sometime — the TVs, the
> phones, the thermostat, the doorbell, the kids' tablets, the speaker in the kitchen.
> Fifteen, twenty, easy. Every one of them is talking to the internet all day long, and a
> surprising amount of what they're saying is junk you'd never agree to out loud: who's
> watching what, where the house is, what the kids searched. None of it is a crisis. It's
> just quietly happening — and almost nobody is keeping an eye on it.
>
> That's the job we do…

Calm, concrete, second-person. No fear, no acronyms.

## Ads (same voice, shorter)

Headlines that have the analogy doing the work:

- "Pest control — but for everything on your Wi-Fi."
- "You pay the bug guy monthly. Your internet deserves the same."
- "We keep the junk off your kids' tablets. You'll never have to think about it."
- "A real local person watching your home network. Background-checked. Bonded."

Each ad is aimed at one neighborhood's ZIPs (see [`geo-targeting.md`](geo-targeting.md)) and
points at the homepage → booking form.

## Getting found in search

- **Local first.** The Google Business listing (01) and a page per neighborhood do the
  high-intent work — the person typing "network help near me" at 9pm.
- **Educational long-tail.** The posts above own the "should I worry about ___" searches.
- **Proof links.** Every post links to the live gallery (`a777ance.github.io/localDNS/`) so
  search and the real artifact reinforce each other.

## Guardrails

- **Never lead with fear.** Lead with the quiet. Fear gets one sale and then churns.
- **Never claim a threat we can't show.** If we can't measure it, we don't say it happened.
- **Always tie back to the statement.** The monthly proof *is* the product — every piece of
  content points at it.
