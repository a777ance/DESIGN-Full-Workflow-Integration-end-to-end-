# Contractor agreement — required sections (outline)

The structure the operator/sub-contractor agreement must cover. **This is an outline, not
legal text** — have counsel draft and review the actual agreement, especially the
classification-sensitive sections ([LAUNCH-NOTES #14](../LAUNCH-NOTES.md#14-worker-misclassification)).
E-signed at onboarding (09); timestamp written to `operator.vetting.agreement_signed_ts`.

---

## Required sections

1. **Parties & relationship** — names the platform and the operator as an **independent
   contractor**, not an employee. (The section counsel must get right — see the
   classification note below.)
2. **Scope of services** — managed home-network service to the operator's own customers;
   the operator controls *how* the work is done (independence preserved).
3. **The guild model & money** — operator pays the platform **dues**; operator bills
   customers **directly**; the platform does **not** take a per-job cut. Mirrors
   `MARKETING`'s money-flow.
4. **Brand & tooling license** — use of the A777ance brand (00), the generator/tooling,
   and the directory, conditioned on good standing and vetting.
5. **Standards & trust** — vetting maintained (09), bonding/insurance kept active, the
   data-handling and honesty rules (no invented figures; aggregate-only privacy; categories
   never domains).
6. **Data & privacy** — handling of customer PII and network data; confidentiality;
   breach obligations.
7. **Tax** — W-9 requirement; 1099-NEC issuance; the operator's responsibility for their
   own taxes.
8. **Term & termination** — at-will; what happens to the operator's book of homes and the
   home-JSONs in `localDNS` on exit (continuity for customers).
9. **IP & confidentiality** — the stack/tooling/brand remain the platform's; customer
   relationships per the guild model.
10. **Liability & indemnification** — `CHANGE_ME`, per counsel.

## The classification caution (read every time)

The agreement can *state* an independent-contractor relationship, but **conduct controls
classification.** If the platform dictates routes, schedules, pricing, and methods, a
regulator can reclassify the operator as an employee regardless of what the paper says.
Keep the operator genuinely independent (they own the *how*), and **confirm the structure
with counsel before scaling.** Documenting this honestly is the point — do not paper over
it.
