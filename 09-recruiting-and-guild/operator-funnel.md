# How a customer becomes an operator

The path from "I'd like to do this" to "running my own block of homes." It's deliberately the
**same warm path as a sale** (02→05), because these aren't strangers — they already live with
the product and trust it.

---

## The path

```
CUSTOMER gets a statement (06)
   │  taps "Connect in the Alliance"  ─►  marked interested (08)
   ▼
APPLICANT  ─► the "become an operator" page (01), or the nudge email (02)
   │  books an interview (same Setmore as a consult, 03)
   ▼
VETTING  ─► background check · bond · references · agreement  (vetting-checklist.md)
   │  status moves: applicant → vetting → active
   ▼
NEW OPERATOR
   │  10: W-9 + signed agreement (before any payment)
   │  09: matched to a route (02), set up with member dues
   ▼
RUNS STATEMENTS for a block of homes  ─► back to 06, now at fleet scale
```

## Why it converts so well

- **They're warm, not cold.** The applicant already uses the box and got value from it. You're
  not convincing them it works — they know.
- **The portfolio is the pitch.** Show them the operator's monthly portfolio
  (`localDNS/docs/statements/operator/`): "this is your dashboard, this is you earning your
  keep." The artifact sells the role better than a recruiter can.
- **Real side income, low fuss.** They keep their own home's protection and add a small
  business. The box and the statement tool make homes #2–#20 profitable instead of exhausting
  (the math is in [`operator-day-one.md`](operator-day-one.md) and `MARKETING`).

## It feeds the density loop (with stage 02)

A new operator gets matched to a **block**, not scattered homes — and a full block with nobody
to serve it is itself a reason to go find an operator. The two loops feed each other: density
makes an operator worth it; an operator makes density serviceable.
