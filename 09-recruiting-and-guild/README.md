## The customer is the recruiting pool

DoorDash had to go find drivers and diners separately. We don't: **a happy customer is an
operator waiting to happen.** They already trust the box, they're a little handy, and a lot of
them want the side income. Jose was customer HH-0007 before he ran a route. So recruiting starts
*inside the statement* — the "Connect in the Alliance" tap — not as some separate cold-hiring
slog. Anyone can be a customer, an operator, or both.

## Why vetting is heavy on purpose

A background-checked, bonded local person is the exact thing the $30-a-year apps can never
copy. **Vetting isn't overhead — it's the product.** One unvetted operator who does something
dumb in someone's home torches the whole trust pitch
([LAUNCH-NOTES #12](../LAUNCH-NOTES.md#12-operator-onboarded-without-vetting)). So the gate is
real — see [`vetting-checklist.md`](vetting-checklist.md).

## The operator and the money

An operator is a real entry on the list (08 → operator): their status (applicant → vetting →
active), the customer they used to be, the routes they cover, their member dues, their vetting,
and their tax info (stage 10). The shape of the deal: operators pay the **platform** a membership
due and **bill their own customers** for the service directly — the platform doesn't take a cut
of every job. The exact dues number is still open (`MARKETING`).

## Hand-offs

- **← 06 statements:** the "Connect in the Alliance" tap creates the operator lead.
- **→ 10 compliance:** nobody gets paid until the W-9 and the agreement are signed.
- **↔ 08 customer list:** the operator and route entries live there.
- **→ 02 geo:** a full block with nobody to serve it is a signal to go recruit.
